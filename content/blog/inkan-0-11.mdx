An inkan app knows a lot about itself: every route, its contract, its examples, its tool
name, its jobs. Until now you saw most of that in the docs page, and the requests in the
inspector at `/_inkan`. In 0.11 the inspector grows into a studio.

## inkan studio

Start the app in development and open `/_inkan`. Five tabs:

- **live**: the requests as they come, with replay and copy as example as before, and above
  them the last minute at a glance: requests, broken answers, p95 and the heap, each with a
  sparkline.
- **routes**: every route with its calls, what broke, p50 and p95 since the studio opened,
  sortable. The slowest route is one click away.
- **map**: the app as a tree of its paths, with the methods, the MCP tools and the routes
  that ask for credentials.
- **mcp**: the tools from 0.10, each with a form to call it as a client would. With MCP off
  it shows what it would serve.
- **jobs**: the queues, the next scheduled run, progress as it happens, a cancel button, and
  the last jobs that ended with their results.

It is a page the app serves itself. No account, no agent, nothing to install, and nothing
on the request path in production: the numbers are only kept while the studio is on.

## protect

Someone asked me whether the docs page can be switched off, and whether it can have a
password. Switching off was always there (`docs: false`, `openapi: false`), but a password
was not, and could not be added from outside: the pages inkan serves itself are answered
before any hook runs, so an auth hook never sees them.

```ts
inkan({ protect: { user: "team", password: env("DOCS_PASSWORD") } });
```

`protect` puts HTTP Basic auth on `/docs`, `/openapi.json` and the studio, compared in
constant time, or takes a function that decides alone. With it the studio also answers
beyond your own machine, so it can run on a server, behind the password. Your routes and the
MCP endpoint keep their own `security`. The docs now have a page on
[what is open in production](/docs/deploy#what-is-open-in-production) and how to close it.

## About speed

The studio is off in production, and nothing it needs runs on the path of a request there.
The first bench said otherwise, by a little: 0.11 was behind 0.10 in all three runs. One
small thing had moved, a request that is not GET left the built-in pages a few comparisons
later than before. With that back as it was, on the same GitHub runner as always, inkan
scores 91.8 (plain `node:http` as 100), 0.10.0 90.9, Fastify 89.6, Hono 85.1 and Express
50.5, the median of three runs. The runs and every scenario are in
[the report](https://github.com/inkanjs/inkan/blob/main/bench/reports/0.11.0.md).
